Primary Atmospheric Pollutants

🌫️
PM2.5 — Fine Particles
Particles smaller than 2.5 micrometers. Most dangerous as they penetrate deep into lungs and bloodstream. Major source: vehicle exhaust, industrial burning, crop burning. WHO safe limit: 15 μg/m³ (annual).
💨
PM10 — Coarse Particles
Particles up to 10 micrometers. Affects respiratory system. Common in construction areas, unpaved roads, and dust storms. WHO safe limit: 45 μg/m³ (annual).
⚗️
NO₂ — Nitrogen Dioxide
Emitted by vehicles, power plants, and industries. Causes respiratory issues and contributes to smog formation. Also creates ground-level ozone. WHO limit: 10 μg/m³ (annual).
🌀
O₃ — Ground-Level Ozone
Formed by chemical reactions between NOx and VOCs in sunlight. Causes coughing, chest pain, worsens asthma. Peaks in hot afternoons. WHO limit: 100 μg/m³ (8-hour).
🔥
SO₂ — Sulfur Dioxide
Produced by burning fossil fuels, especially coal and oil. Causes acid rain, irritates respiratory tract. Major sources: power plants, steel mills. WHO limit: 40 μg/m³ (24-hour).
⚠️
CO — Carbon Monoxide
Odorless, colorless gas from incomplete combustion. Reduces blood oxygen capacity. High indoor concentrations from gas heaters or cooking fires are especially dangerous. WHO limit: 4 mg/m³ (24-hour).

Vulnerability Guidelines

👶 Children & Infants

Children breathe more air per body weight than adults and are more vulnerable. Keep children indoors when AQI exceeds 100. Ensure proper ventilation in schools and homes. Avoid outdoor sports during smog season (Oct–Feb in Punjab).

🫁 Asthma & Respiratory Patients

Keep rescue inhalers accessible at all times. Monitor AQI daily. When AQI exceeds 150, consult your doctor about adjusting medication. N95 masks provide significant protection.

🧓 Elderly Population

Older adults with heart or lung disease are at highest risk. Avoid outdoor activities during early morning when PM2.5 peaks. Use HEPA air purifiers in living spaces. Stay hydrated and maintain indoor humidity 40–60%.

🏃 General Public

When AQI is 0–100, outdoor activities are generally safe. When AQI exceeds 150, limit strenuous outdoor activity. Consider carpooling and using public transport to help reduce emissions.

🌿 Mitigation Strategies for Pakistan

  • Use public transport or carpool — vehicle emissions are the #1 source of urban air pollution
  • Avoid crop burning — it causes severe smog events especially in Punjab (Oct–Nov)
  • Support clean energy — push for solar and wind energy adoption across cities
  • Plant trees — urban greenery captures PM2.5 and cools city temperatures
  • Reduce indoor burning — wood, coal, and kerosene burning pollutes indoor air severely
  • Report illegal industrial emissions to EPA via the Green Complaints app
